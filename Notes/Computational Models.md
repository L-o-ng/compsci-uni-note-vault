A **Model** is something that attempts to capture important aspects of $X$ in a surrogate $Y$.
A **Computational Model** is a formally described set of processes capturing aspects of how something functions.

Computational models allow us to draw conclusions about the things modelled:
+ *Predictions*, such as "Who will win the next election?";
+ *Explanations*, such as: "why did a societal change happen *here*?";

In the context of computational social science, models are used to inform policy and governmental decision-making.
## Difficulties
Some things are hard to model. This is because they may be:
+ Not a simple function of information easily accessible **prior** to the fact;
+ Not simply random or fundamentally unpredictable;
+ Very often constrained by the **availability** of data;
+ Represented by countless hidden variables and factors.
## Types of Model
+ A **causal** model attempts to model the mechanisms that lead to observed outcomes.
+ A **predictive** model may not *directly* model these mechanisms.

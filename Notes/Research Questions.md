Research is important for *knowledge* **discovery** and **creation**. It is a means to *understand* issues, identify *opportunities*, and sift **fact** from **fiction**. It is the basis of *innovation*.
> [!abstract] 
> A good research question is:
> + **Focused** and **Researchable**:
> 	+ Focuses on a single topic and problem;
> 	+ Answerable using only primary/secondary data;
> 	+ Does not ask for a subjective value judgement;
> 	+ Does not ask why.
> + **Feasible** and **Specific**:
> 	+ Answerable within practical constraints;
> 	+ Uses specific, well-defined concepts;
> 	+ Does not ask for a conclusive solution, policy, or course of action.
> + **Complex** and **Arguable**:
> 	+ Cannot be answered with yes or no;
> 	+ Cannot be answered with easily found facts/figures;
> 	+ Provides scope for debate/deliberation.
> + **Relevant** and **Original**:
> 	+ Addresses a problem relevant to your field/discipline;
> 	+ Contributes to a topical social/academic debate;
> 	+ Has not already been answered.
## Identifying Research Problems
1. Research the broad topic;
2. Research around the topic specifically to find problems;
3. Narrow the topic to a reasonable scenario;
4. Form a research *question* based on the scenario.
### Problem Statement
Having identified a specific problem area, write a **problem statement**.
It should include specifics such as:
+ Context;
+ Specific issue being investigated;
+ Why this problem? Why now?;
+ Set **objectives**.

Write this early on, and restate if it is found to be too vague.
### Characteristics of a Good Problem
+ Focused on a single issue;
+ Researchable using primary/secondary sources;
+ Feasible to answer within the timeframe and practical constraints;
+ Specific enough to answer thoroughly;
+ Complex enough to develop the answer over the space of a paper/thesis;
+ Relevant to your field of study/society more broadly.
### Types of Research Question

| Type          | Formulation                                                                                   |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Descriptive   | What are the characteristics of $x$?                                                          |
| Comparative   | What are the similarities/differences between $x,y$?                                          |
| Correlational | What is the relationship between $x,y$?                                                       |
| Exploratory   | What are the main factors in $x$? What is the role of $y$ in $z$?                             |
| Explanatory   | Does $x$ have an effect on $y$? What is the impact of $y$ on $z$? What are the causes of $x$? |
| Evaluation    | What are the dis/advantages of $x$? How well does $y$ work? How effective/desirable is $z$?   |
| Action        | How can $x$ be achieved? What are the most effective strategies to improve $y$?               |

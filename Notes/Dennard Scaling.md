#notes 

1. **Classical Promise (1974)**: Shrinking MOSFET dimensions by $s$ while reducing supply voltage $V_{dd}$ by $\frac{1}{s}$ kept chip power density constant ($\frac{P}{\text{Area}}\approx 1$);
2. **Free Lunch Era**: Smaller transistors allowed clock frequencies to scale by $s$ and densities by $s^{2}$ without increasing operating temperatures;
3. **Voltage Wall (c2005)**: Threshold voltage $V_{th}$ hit subthreshold leakage limits, halting $V_{dd}$ scaling around $1V$ to preserve noise margins;
4. **Power Wall**: Transistor counts kept growing via *Moore's Law*, but static power leakage and constant $V_{dd}$ caused power density to skyrocket;
5. **Architectural Shift**: Forced the end of frequency scaling, driving the transition to multicore architecture.

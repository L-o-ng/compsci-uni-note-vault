> [!abstract] 
> **Critique** is a somewhat formal word that typically refers to a careful judgement in which *someone* gives an opinion about *something*. **Review** can refer to an essay analysing a literary or artistic work, but can also sometimes *imply* a more casual or personal opinion.

Critique is important to determine which sources to *trust*.
It is a **formal process**, with an *approach*, *structure*, and *writing style* of its own:
1. Introduction;
2. Body (summary of work and evaluation);
3. Conclusion.

A critique is a *deep dive* into a **single** body of work: *not* a literature review.
## Parts
### Introduction
We introduce:
+ The work;
+ The context in which it was produced;
+ The contribution;

And have a single sentence describing your findings of the critique.
### Body
#### Summary
We *summarise* the main points of the article, and describe how the author describes these.
#### Evaluation
We ask *critical*, *objective* questions to explore a **systematic** and **thorough** assessment of the work presented. An author will have a *goal*, a *topic*, a *context*, a *methodology*, some *data*, a *result*, etc: all of which they need to argue for. An objective analysis of their success in achieving them will drive the critique.

Start your critique by asking questions, such as:  
+ Has the author been objective about their work?
+ Have the aims been clearly described, do they make sense, have they been achieved?
+ Are there any assumptions (stated or implied), are they valid, do they undermine the work?
+ What evidence is given to support he findings, does the authors argument hold water?
+ Is the work well structured, does each section support the next?
### Conclusion
Summarise the evaluation, and describe how you reached the summation.
